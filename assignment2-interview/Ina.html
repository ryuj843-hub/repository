short version

Interviewer
I thought it would be very interesting to know what their
childhood looked like. So, let's start. Can you introduce
yourself?
Ina
Hi, I'm Ina. I'm a student, and I'm interested in
animation, drawing, writing, and creating things.
Interviewer
Can you introduce your childhood?
Ina
When I was a kid, I preferred the boys' toy section.
I liked dinosaurs, dragons, horses, and weird creatures.
I was really obsessed with How to Train Your Dragon
and My Little Pony. I didn't really like fake content
that was centered around people.
Interviewer
What are the most important things in your life right now?
Ina
My projects, my family, my dog Lulu, my friends,
and drawing and writing are really important to me.
Interviewer
What is your favorite color?
Ina
I don't really have one favorite color. I really like
the sky during golden hour, especially when it has
that gradient of red and yellow.
Interviewer
What are your favorite hobbies or activities?
Ina
I like kayaking and being around water. I also enjoy
hiking, baking, trying new food combinations, drawing,
comics, shows, films, and listening to movie soundtracks
and instrumental music.
Interviewer
Why did you choose Parsons?
Ina
I could picture myself there, and the community felt right.
It's also a strong school, and being in New York gives
me opportunities in design and creative work. It's also
relatively close to my family.
Interviewer
What was your favorite thing to do as a child?
Ina
I loved drawing and making little books. I would staple
pages together and create keyframes and stories inside them.
Interviewer
What was your favorite childhood TV show?
Ina
My favorite was The Amazing World of Gumball. I found
it through cable TV and Cartoon Network, and later
watched it on my iPad.
Interviewer
What was your favorite childhood book?
Ina
I really liked Wings of Fire. My brother introduced
me to it. It was pretty violent, but I really
enjoyed the series.
Interviewer
Were there any challenges while you were growing up?
Ina
A lot of the challenges were social and internal. I
struggled with expressing myself, how I viewed myself,
how I treated myself, and friendships. My school community
was also mostly nonexistent, so I often felt isolated.
Interviewer
What are your opinions on people getting married and having children?
Ina
I support people choosing marriage and children if that's
what they want, but I don't think people should be pressured
into it. I'm not sure whether I want children myself.
My career is a priority, and pregnancy makes me uncomfortable,
although that could change in the future.
Interviewer
Do you ever think that childhood is a crucial point in your life?
Ina
I think childhood is foundational, but I don't think
it completely determines who someone becomes. Adulthood can
change a person depending on their experiences, time,
energy, and support.
Interviewer
Have you ever had an existential crisis while growing up?
Ina
I had them during freshman year of high school and
during my first semester of college. The college one
was more intense because everything happened at once.
I was in a new place without my usual support system,
meeting new people, and having more control over my life.
Interviewer
What other commentary would you like to tell me?
Ina
I think I've grown physically and mentally, but not
in a straight line. Time is fascinating because difficult
periods can feel very slow, but then suddenly they're over.
I find some comfort in knowing that everything passes.
Interviewer
Thanks for participating.
