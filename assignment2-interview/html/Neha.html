Interviewer
I thought it would be very interesting to know what their childhood
looked like. So, let's start. Can you introduce yourself?
Neha
Hi, I'm Neha. I'm an economics major at NYU, and I'm from
South India, specifically Hyderabad.
Interviewer
Can you introduce your childhood?
Neha
I spent a lot of my childhood between spaces. I lived in
New Jersey for a bit, then we moved to Seattle, then we
moved to a state in South India called Bville, and then I
shifted to Hyderabad. So there was a lot of shifting, and even
if I was in the same place, I was shifting between schools.
I was also a very shy person, so it was a lot
of adjusting and learning about new places.
Interviewer
What are the most important things in your life right now?
Neha
Because I just turned 20, I would say the most important thing
right now is learning about myself and trying to understand myself. I've
learned to stand up for myself more, to confront people immediately, and
to create new interests for myself. I'm exploring things outside my comfort
zone. Doing all of that is important to shape my identity because
I'm 20. After that, obviously, my friends and my family are very
important to me.
Interviewer
What is your favorite color?
Neha
I think it's yellow. My parents loved the color yellow growing up,
but I hated it because I thought it looked bad on my
skin. But now I really like it. I also like how it
can spark joy, like sunlight, so I just like yellow.
Interviewer
Would you say you're a shy person at this point?
Neha
It depends. If I'm surrounded by a lot of shy people, then
I'm not shy. But if I'm surrounded by a lot of confident
people who already know each other, then I get very shy. I
tend to get very shy, especially meeting someone I don't know.

I don't think I had a lot of friends from when I

I don't think I have many from my childhood.

I did.

me think more, so sometimes I don't do them. I also like
eating. I like just walking in the park and maybe drinking something.

I'm definitely more introverted than extroverted. I do like small talk with
people and stuff, but I just don't like talking to people sometimes.

It kind of depends. Depending on my condition or circumstances, I could

My mom would usually give me a slice of bread, some pizza

I didn't watch one specific show during my childhood. My brother is

It was always within India. We explored every part of India, and

When we went to the mountains, there was a road going up

any jackets or anything like that.

Did you get to see the locals there?

Yeah, I did. Most of them were different. They had a lot

I always wanted to be a teacher of economics or just a

I think that was it.

I liked Nancy Drew and mystery books, like The Secret Seven and

Dr. Seuss books. Those were my favorite childhood books.
Interviewer
Do you remember your childhood friends? How did you have fun during

Neha
In New Jersey, I had my neighborhood best friend, and we would

movies. Then I had some other friends who were nearby, and we

Interviewer
When did you have your first turning point in your life, if

Neha
I don't remember exactly. I had a turning point when I was

Interviewer

Neha
I think it's more constructive.
Interviewer
What do you remember about your parents? Has your opinion changed after
you moved out?
Neha
When I was living with them, I was just seeing it from
my perspective. They were my parents and they were raising me. When
I moved out, I was able to see them getting older and
look at them objectively, like from a third person's point of view.
While I was living with them, because I was constantly interacting with
them, I wasn't able to question some things they said to me.
Once I moved out, I was able to see from a third
person's point of view why they did that and who they are
as people besides my parents.
Interviewer
How would you describe your parents in three words?
Neha
Anxious, clueless, and hardworking.
Interviewer
Why those words?
Neha
Because they grew up with a lot. For example, my grandparents had
the same level of wealth throughout their lives. But for my parents,
their wealth grew exponentially because of the tech industry and everything. They
didn't know a lot about how to navigate my college life and
things like that. So they don't know much, which is why I
said clueless. I said anxious because they don't know much, they're anxious
about everything, and they're always teaching more precaution. And I said hardworking
because all they knew was to work hard so that they could
make sure they worked as hard as they could.
Interviewer
Were they kind of violent to you?
Neha
Not really. Even when they had me, no one really taught them
about being friends with their children, so everything they did with me,
they were doing for the first time. Compared to my brother, when
they had more experience, they didn't know how to socialize a lot
of things with me, and even now.
Interviewer
So you're the first child to them, so they must have paid
so much attention or had expectations for you, right?
Neha
Yes.
Interviewer
Do you remember anything related to that?
Neha
Yes. In India, people usually do engineering or they become people in
medical school. My dad didn't get into medical school. He got into
the second best and he was really disappointed. So during my childhood,
he would put the expectation of getting into medical school on me.
Interviewer
When you decided to move out for NYU, how did you think
your dad would feel?
Neha
He was really proud because I was the first person in my
family, I guess besides my uncle, who got into a good university
internationally. He was really proud. I remember he would send me messages
saying, 'Oh my God, I'm so proud of you,' and all that.
I was so weirded out by it because he's never said things
like that before.
Interviewer
What are your hobbies that you like to achieve something in?
Neha
I've only done my sweaters and crocheting. I feel like if I've
been successful at all, at least I think crocheting might mean I
can stop spending money. I think crocheting takes a lot of effort
and time.

Neha
I don't feel relaxed. It's more like I try to feel relaxed.
I'm crocheting and watching a video or something, but then I need
to make sure the stitches are good and I'm not messing up.
So it's like a mental exercise.
Interviewer
Were there any challenges while you were growing up?
Neha
I don't think there were any major challenges, but I depend on
our wealth levels, the stability, and my parents. All of those things
are better now than they were when I was growing up.
Interviewer
What are your opinions on people getting married and having children?
Neha
I feel like that seems like the answer to life, but it
doesn't have to be. Having a kid is so, honestly, if you
just stay alone instead of spending money on the kid and the
adult, that would be great too, and you could have so much
time to try and be happier in your life. Some people think
having kids is fulfilling. I feel like when I'm 30 I might
feel that, but right now I don't know if I do. I
think not enough people question it. At least I don't question it
enough, especially because my mom thinks that's the answer. She says there's
a purpose with having a kid, which I think is really sad.
Interviewer
Why do you think you might change your mind when you're 30?
Neha
I feel like I would feel social pressure from getting married or
having kids. From now onwards to 30, my life is going to
be very busy. I'm going to be going to Denmark and have
this and that, but I think at 30 life would slow down
and then I'm going to start thinking more. I'm going to realize
that there's no perfect life and then I'm going to have to
create a purpose, which is good. Also, I think as I grow
older, I'll feel a little bit more lonely, especially when I'm 50
or something, and I think having a kid would help with that.
Interviewer

Neha
I think adoption is a good idea because you give more kids

kid.
